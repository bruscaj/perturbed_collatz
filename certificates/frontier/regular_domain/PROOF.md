# Regular sufficient domains, bounded entry and proof-language complexity

Let `H=(0,0,0,1,0,0,1)`, `z=n+1-H[p]>0`, and phases be modulo seven. The normalized update is `(m z+s)/2`, `s=z mod 2`, `m=3` when `s=H[p]` (G, growing), and `m=1` otherwise (S, shrinking). Write `B(t)=(6t,1)`, `t>=1`. A common future means equal normalized value and phase, possibly at different elapsed clocks. Labels are constant along physical forward edges and hence on inverse ancestors.

## 1. Constructive finite synchronization

For any positive integer D, the entire block `B(t),...,B(t+D-1)` merges on some infinite progression `t=Mv+r`, `M=2^k`, `1<=r<=M`, `v>=0`. The first tick gives phase-two values `9(t+j)`; their free coefficients are odd. After a parity refinement every subsequent affine coefficient is odd and positive, and all slopes have ratios that are powers of three. At each stage use the exact affine pair mechanism from PR24, then preserve all earlier mergers while merging another member. Here is the termination argument for that mechanism:

A canonical pair is `(x,3^e x+b)`, `e>=0`, and `b>=0` if `e=0`. If `s=x mod 2` and `r'=(s+b) mod 2`, one step gives ratio `a'=n*3^e/m`, offset `b'=(n*b+r'-a'*s)/2`, canonicalizing orientation if necessary. For `e>0`, if `b` is odd, select the branch making the lower-slope side grow, decreasing e. If b is even, use `2b'=m*b+s*(1-3^e)`; with `l=v2(3^e-1)`, choose a parity entering the range `v2(b)<l`, and each subsequent update below l decreases that valuation. Thus an odd b is attainable in finite time. At e=0, halve positive even b by shrinking both sides. For odd b>=7, take one tick to e=1, offset <=(3b+3)/2 in magnitude. While the offset is even and has magnitude >=4, choose shrinking branches on both sides, giving `b'=b/2-s`; magnitude decreases. At even offsets 0 or +/-2 select s making the new offset odd of magnitude <=3. Then take an exponent-decreasing step. The new equal-slope gap is <=(3b+5)/4<b. At gaps 1..5 use the 35 terminal words from PR24, independently checked in the constructor's signed affine replay. This is a constructive choice of a residue for a finite cylinder, not control over the orbit of an arbitrary given integer.

Every step selects the next free bit uniquely; the final common affine endpoint has form `(A v+c,p)` with A a positive power of three, c>=1. To align the checkpoint to phase three, take zero to six chosen shrinking steps to phase two, then choose a growing step at phase two. Its output is 0 modulo 3, and A is divisible by 3. All these choices are obtained by finite dyadic refinements. The original block still merges on the resulting progression, now with p=3.

## 2. Direct fixed-clock modular steering and interval steering

At phase three an all-shrink inverse lap is `E(z)=128z-118`, with center `a0=118/127`. An inverse lap with exactly one growing inverse has form `J(z)=(128z-N)/3`, with the seven possible N equal to `162,130,114,138,118,116,119`. A legal inverse is positive: the always-legal shrink inverse is `2z-(1-H[q])`; the grow inverse is `(2z-H[q])/3` when integral. Final integrality of a word implies all intermediate integrality (an uncancelled ternary denominator cannot disappear). The nine-residue normalization table in `verify.py` chooses one such lap sending each z to a positive `z1` with `z1-a0` a 3-adic unit. Every one-grow lap has centered intercept `K=(128/3-1)a0-N/3` of absolute value <16. In particular every positive z has a legal one-grow lap, and the fixed-clock normalizer uses exactly one grow.

For desired endpoint modulus `A=3^a` choose any one-grow lap J legal on z1. Put `G=E^2`, multiplier `B=128^2`, `v3(B-1)=1`. All `G^u(z1)` agree modulo 3, so J is legal for every u>=0. Write lambda=128/3. The centered intercept of J has v3(K)=-1 because `lambda*(z1-a0)` has that valuation and J(z1)-a0 is integral in Z_3. Choose a total number `T=A-1+pad` of G laps, and slide the J lap:

```
G^u, J, G^(T-u), 0<=u<A.
z_u-a0 = lambda B^T (z1-a0) + B^(T-u) K.
```

For distinct u,w, LTE gives `v3(z_u-z_w)=v3(u-w)`, so these A outputs cover all residues modulo A. A ternary digit lift finds the unique split u without enumerating A choices; each candidate can be evaluated modulo 3A with modular powers. The final pad can be chosen uniformly so `a0+B^pad(1-a0)>=c`, hence the selected endpoint lies in `A v+c`, v>=0. For example choose pad>=1 large enough that `B^pad>=127(c+1)/9`. Including normalization, clock `14(T+1)` and grow count two are independent of z. Common slope `s=lambda^2 B^T`. Because all centered intercepts have absolute value <16, the exact real formula satisfies `|z_u/s-z|<2`; the extra block-anchor conversion gives `t=(M*s/A)(z+epsilon)` with `|epsilon|<4` (pad may be increased to ensure s>=A+c). This is a direct small-specialization of the sliding-pump idea in the earlier general fixed-clock theorem.

Align any target of height Z to phase three using <=6 shrink inverses, obtaining z0 with Z<=z0<=64Z. Let C=z0-a0>=9/127. Take j all-shrink inverse laps and then g one-grow inverse laps. The latter exist on every residue, and centered normalized intercepts give

`w=a0+lambda^g(128^j C+e)`, `|e|<=16/(lambda-1)<1`.

Follow with the fixed-clock adapter into the synchronized endpoint progression. The block anchor is

`t=K_D lambda^g (128^j C+eta)`, `K_D=M*s/A`, `|eta|<6`.

For fixed delta>0, the fractional parts of `-g log_128 3` are dense. Find a finite multiplicative net of maximum circular ratio <=1+delta/16 (reduce delta to <=1 if necessary), using exact comparison of rational powers or the standard irrational-rotation proof. Choose J large enough that `6/(128^J*(9/127))<delta/64`. For X at least a constant times `K_D lambda^G 128^J Z` and at least `16D/delta`, choose `g<=G`, `j>=J` using this net so the leading scale is in a small interior interval of `(X,(1+delta)X)`. The relative error is bounded by delta/64. Therefore the entire D-block lies inside the desired interval. Every member reaches the constructed endpoint and then the specified target. This establishes uniform proportional interval entry into a synchronized block, with an effective threshold `X>=C_{D,delta} Z`, without any inverse-density claim. When X/Z is bounded above by a fixed constant, the inverse clock is bounded too (j,g bounded, all adapters fixed).

## 3. Regular sufficiency, bounded entry, and a weaker nonregular target

Take any integer base b>=2 and a DFA for a set S of positive integers of positive upper natural density, reading canonical digits most significant first. (Reverse the language first if supplied LSD-first.) Some accessible closed SCC of the complete transition graph contains an accepting state: otherwise acceptance probability among uniformly chosen digits tends to zero (finite Markov chain transience). Fix a nonzero leading prefix h entering that SCC. A finite irreducible periodic graph has a common sufficiently large path length k to an accepting state from each state in a chosen cyclic class, when the length has the correct residue mod the period. For infinitely many lengths ell in a fixed arithmetic progression, every intermediate word of length ell-k ends in that class. Thus every aligned b^k-block of `[h b^ell,(h+1)b^ell)` contains an S member.

Use the direct interval steering theorem with D=2*b^k and an interior fixed proportional band. Every target has a synchronized block of ancestors in this cone and hence an ancestor in S. Choose the smallest allowed length ell for which the band is beyond the threshold proportional to Z. Successive allowed lengths differ by a constant, so the chosen band is still within a bounded multiple of Z. Therefore there are constants `L_S,C_S` such that every target of height Z has a B1 ancestor parameter t in S with t<=C_S Z and clock<=L_S. This is all-base regular sufficiency, independent of probabilistic recurrence or the analytic linear inverse floor. In particular proof of capture on S alone suffices. More generally bounded gaps on infinitely many fixed proportional intervals suffice, without assuming S regular.

One can also allow a slowly growing block width. For fixed delta define explicit effective monotone thresholds `K_D=max_{j<=D}(C_{j,delta},16j/delta, j)` via the preceding construction. Let `D(X)=max{D: K_D<=sqrt(X)}` (or 1 if empty). Then D(X) tends to infinity. A set hitting every D(X)-member consecutive block inside infinitely many fixed proportional intervals `(X,(1+delta)X)` contains an ancestor of every fixed target (eventually Z<=sqrt(X)). This may have density zero. A crude quantitative bound follows from the greedy synchronization proof: after l literal ticks, exponent e<=l and log(1+|b|)=O(l+log(D+1)); reducing e costs O(l log(l+2)) ticks, since `v2(3^e-1)<=2+log2(e)`. Then log(1+|b|)=O(l log(l+2)+log D). Equal-slope gap descent uses O(log(1+b)) excursions, each O(log(1+b)) ticks. Thus each added member costs at most `C(l+log(D+1)+1)^4`, giving total l<=exp(exp(C'D)) for D>=2. Modulus A<=3^(l+2), M<=2^l, adapter clock O(A+log(c+1)), and c<=exp(O(l+log D)), hence `K_D<=exp(exp(exp(exp(C_delta D))))` with suitably large effective constant. In particular a sufficiently small constant times fourth iterated logarithm of X is a sufficient growing width for large X. The effective threshold definition is the safer primary statement. If importing PR24's written quantitative growing-block bound (15), a much better conditional bound on l by a power of log D follows; it was not needed for the unconditional constructive result. Neither bound says the known basin has such short gaps.

## 4. Explicit bounded TM interface

`S_TM={t>=1: popcount(t) is even}` is binary regular with two states, density 1/2. It contains no whole infinite AP: given step d>0, choose odd m with 2^m>d; `d(2^m-1)` has popcount exactly m, by splitting `(d-1)2^m+(2^m-d)`. Adding a sufficiently high binary shift of this multiple of d flips digit parity without a carry from the original fixed term. Hence every infinite AP meets both parities.

The whole-affine pair `t=256v+252`, t+1, reaches `(81v+81,3)` at clock 9. One is in S_TM. The finite inverse search in `fixed_block_bank` covers all 6561 target residues at phase three with at most 35 inverse ticks (<=4 grows), and phase alignment adds <=6, giving entry <=50 total.

The optimized selector groups source forms `t=2^k v+r`, `0<=r<2^k`, after `k+1` ticks by common endpoint `3^a v+c` and phase, retaining even and odd low-prefix digit sums. Different clocks are allowed; the high binary tail v is unchanged in each source form. If both low-prefix parities are represented, one of the two positive launches lies in S_TM for every v. For r=0 require v>=1. The dynamic program refines `v=2w+d`, updates r to r+2^k*d, and applies the exact branch to the endpoint. Exponent cap 9 and k<=29 suffice. This is a finite certificate generator, not a claim that the retained bank is globally optimal. Independent signed affine replay of both sides of each of 55,008 selected pair rows checks complete target coverage on modulus 3^9 at each phase. 4,407 rows have unequal clocks. The uniform parameter bound is t<= (2^22/81) Z and maximum clock 30. Each affine endpoint has nonnegative offset relative to its homogeneous source slope, so the height bound holds for all lifts, not just residues. No capture of all S_TM has been proved.
